---
title: Your first task
description: How product people, engineers, non-technical founders, and AI agents can start using PDI.
---

Once PDI is installed, you do not need special syntax to start. Describe **who** the experience is for, **what** problem you are solving, **constraints**, and **what outcome** you want.

## Examples by role

### Product Manager / Non-technical Founder
> "Review our onboarding flow for first-time users. Identify the main usability issues and suggest the smallest changes that would help. Keep the existing sign-in provider."

### Engineer / Developer
> "Use `design-system` and `ui-prototyping` to build a settings form for notification preferences using our existing shadcn/ui components. Show loading, empty, and error states."

### Designer
> "Run a `ux-audit` on our checkout page. Prioritize findings by severity (Critical, Major, Minor) and propose concrete copy and layout fixes."

## What to expect from your assistant

When PDI is active, your assistant will:
1. **Understand before building**: clarify the user problem and constraints instead of jumping straight to generic UI.
2. **Separate facts from assumptions**: tell you what it verified in your files versus what it inferred.
3. **Propose the smallest effective change**: avoid unnecessary complexity, extra modals, or bloated components.

## Review the response

Check that the assistant:
- Clearly explains the problem and trade-offs
- Points to real files or constraints it inspected
- Surfaces any assumptions or open questions

PDI provides structured guidance; **you** remain responsible for deciding what to keep and what to ship.
