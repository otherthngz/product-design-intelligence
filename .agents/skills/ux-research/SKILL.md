# Ux Research

## Purpose and use
Plan user research, usability tests, interview guides and synthesize supplied research notes or transcripts into tagged evidence. Use when planning discovery or synthesizing supplied observations; do not use to invent participant quotes or replace real user research.

## Inputs
Required: the product decision or research question, plus any available transcripts, tickets, or observation notes. Optional: audience, recruitment constraints, existing product flows, and business goals. State missing material and assumptions; ask when a consequential decision cannot be made safely.

## Relevant knowledge
Load only what the task needs: [research synthesis](../../../knowledge/ux-design/research-synthesis.md), [outcomes](../../../knowledge/product-design/outcomes.md). Choose depth based on context; see the [topic index](../../../knowledge/index.md).

## Workflow
1. Identify the product decision that needs evidence, the target audience, and what material is already supplied versus unknown.
2. For research planning: define research objectives, participant screening criteria, non-leading interview questions or realistic task scenarios, observation metrics, and ethical/privacy boundaries.
3. For research synthesis: extract observed behaviors and direct evidence from supplied transcripts, tickets, or analytics. Tag every claim with its exact source ID and confidence (**user-provided**, **verified**, **assumption**, or **hypothesis**).
4. Group findings by user task and obstacle; state sample limitations, disconfirming evidence, and prioritized recommendations.
5. Route validated findings to `product-framing`, `interaction-design`, or `ux-audit` when the user wants next-stage design deliverables.

## Artifacts
Produce a research plan, usability test script, or evidence synthesis report in Markdown. Apply [the output contract](../../../workflows/output-contract.md) at the task's complexity level. Link actual files and distinguish findings from hypotheses.

## Quality
Check coverage against the request, non-leading question phrasing, traceable source citations, explicit sample limits, and honest validation. Use [design guidance](../../../knowledge/design-practice.md) and the [review rubric](../../../tests/evaluation-cases.md) as needed.

## Failure modes
Avoid fabricated participant quotes, invented sample sizes or percentages, leading questions that prime the desired answer, and treating a single user opinion as population consensus. Do not silently skip supplied disconfirming evidence.

## Tools and fallback
Use available local files and appropriate inspection tools to read supplied transcripts or data. If raw research material is missing, do not invent findings—deliver a structured research plan or synthesis template and state the gap. Obtain approval before destructive, irreversible, publishing or production-impacting actions.

## Example
A usability test plan with five non-leading task prompts for workspace setup, or a synthesis of four supplied interview notes mapping observed hesitation on permissions to a concrete framing recommendation.
